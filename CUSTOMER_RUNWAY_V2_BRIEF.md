# Customer-first Assessment Runway v2

## First-principles conclusion

A customer does **not** open this experience to inspect value domains, learn evidence-gate vocabulary, navigate a discovery repository, or calculate ROI. The customer has a much simpler job: decide whether it is sensible to spend one more conversation on OTM, identify the one transport reality worth discussing first, and know exactly who and what to bring to that conversation.

The present Runway incorrectly exposes the internal execution sequence — Driver → Discovery → Evidence Gate → ROI — as customer navigation. Those concepts remain essential for the consulting team, but must run in the background. Customers should never need to choose an Evidence Gate or find an ROI workspace.

## The customer promise

> In three minutes, leave with a clear answer to three questions: **Is there a credible reason to explore OTM now? Where should we start? What should happen next?**

The flow must not recommend a product, forecast ROI, claim implementation readiness, or create a buying commitment. It creates a **bounded next conversation**, not a business case.

| Customer receives | What it means in plain language | What stays internal |
|---|---|---|
| **A clear starting point** | One operating issue or transport flow worth examining first | The mapped OTM value driver and all wider library relationships |
| **A simple readiness picture** | What is already visible and what still needs a short conversation | Evidence Gate E0–E3, methodology rules and ROI eligibility |
| **A 45-minute next-step brief** | Who should join, what they should discuss, and a short data/information checklist | Discovery-file detail, customer evidence register, assumptions and owner controls |

## Customer interaction model

The client starts with one of three familiar statements: **“We are concerned about freight bills and spend,” “We struggle to see and respond to shipments,” or “We have another transport or trade issue.”** They then answer six plain-language questions one at a time. Every answer advances the flow. There are no dead-end buttons, no long page jumps, no modules to interpret, and no requirement to know OTM language.

After the sixth answer, the customer sees a single result page. The page contains a one-sentence outcome, three visual signals (operating need, information trail, ability to convene), and a ready-to-share “next conversation card.” The card names the recommended 45-minute conversation, the two people to invite, the first topic, and the minimum information to bring. The customer may copy or download this brief.

## Presentation architecture

The public entry should lead to the customer journey. The existing Decision Archive remains available as an explicitly labelled **internal workbench**, not as a destination that clients must navigate. The former Assessment Runway in the workbench becomes a quiet internal handoff/status bar, because its detailed steps matter to consultants only after the customer conversation produces a starting point.

| Surface | Audience | Main action | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public entry / client flow** | Customer | Answer six questions | Exploration map + next-conversation brief |
| **Client result** | Customer and consultant | Copy, save or share the brief | A bounded agenda, not an ROI promise |
| **Decision Archive workbench** | Presales and consulting | Open the linked driver and evidence file | Controlled Discovery, Evidence Gate and ROI work |

## Result states

The result is intentionally not a score. It uses three decision states that tell a customer what to do next.

1. **Credible place to start** — the problem, initial information trail and people are sufficiently visible for a 45-minute exploration.
2. **Make the next conversation smaller** — there is a reason to explore, but the first flow, region, operating issue or team must be narrowed.
3. **Clarify the operating problem first** — name a real transport concern and where it occurs before discussing software.

## Design contract

The customer flow keeps the single-blue **Cobalt Field Guide** identity already established in Client Brief v2. It should use one calm screen at a time, a visible progress route, answers that advance immediately, and an illustrated final map. The internal Decision Archive keeps its ivory, charcoal and vermilion dossier system, but is no longer the primary customer interaction surface.
