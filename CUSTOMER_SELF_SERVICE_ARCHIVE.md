# Customer Self-Service Decision Archive

## Role correction

The person using the Decision Archive is the organisation assessing its own transportation operation. They are not a salesperson preparing an external client meeting, and the application must never narrate their situation from the outside as “the customer.”

> The archive should say **your operation, your question, your information and your internal discussion**.

## Keep

The Decision Archive visual language, the value-domain library, the evidence discipline, and the no-ROI-before-E2/E3 boundary remain useful for a customer. They help a customer make a bounded, evidence-led decision without a sales pitch.

## Rewrite

| Existing language pattern | Customer self-service language |
|---|---|
| Customer problem / client conversation | Your transport question / the issue in your operation |
| Customer working session | Your 45-minute internal discussion |
| Ask the client to bring | Gather from your team |
| Invite | Include colleagues who know the process, payments and systems |
| Internal Workbench | Decision Archive / OTM exploration workspace |
| Customer or executive output | A discussion brief for your team |
| Customer baseline and owner validation | Your baseline and the colleague who can verify it |

## Customer task outcome

The first Freight Audit task should produce a **Freight Audit Discussion Brief**. It tells the user: the issue they selected, the three pieces of information to locate, the colleagues to involve, and a 45-minute internal agenda. It deliberately does not claim ROI, recommend OTM, or require the user to understand Discovery, Evidence Gates or Business Case terminology.

## Language rule

The visible product language should be in second person and should describe the user’s own context. Evidence and ROI mechanics may retain concise technical labels where needed, but each technical section must explain why it helps the user decide what to do next.
